# The Island's Living Systems

For every lore premise, trace: historical cause -> resource/occupation changes -> two NPC perspectives -> shop price or contract -> authored quest -> visible city change -> long-term payoff. Ask who imports paper, glue, binding thread and dye; who lends books; why family chronicle matters; how the town survives, not just how scenery looks.

Economy requires sources, sinks, safety nets and meaningful choices; no mandatory real-world shop debt countdown or medical-care gating for elder. Weather/day/season should affect believable supply and NPC scheduling without turning play into chores. Separate normal fast shelf sales from curated repairs and archive dilemmas. Give characters conflicting agendas and optional humor; avoid generic fetch quests and copycat story scenes.

Judge by player-readable consequences, original authored character stories, coherent resource costs and nonpunitive recovery. No real book file/content/title in in-world catalog. Use external GitHub writing skills as critique methods, not higher authority than user-approved canon.
