# Veil Reader — Settlement Progression Bible 0.1

## Fantasy
The player begins alone in a dead, wind-scoured wasteland. Reading does not merely increase a number: it brings Water, knowledge, materials, people and memory back into the land. Over a long reading life the same barren place can become a camp, farmstead, village, fortified settlement, castle and living city.

## Core loop
**Read → earn evidence-backed resources → make a meaningful settlement choice → world visibly changes → new stories/systems unlock → return to reading.**

The world must never progress from rapid tapping. Rewards are based on durable reading sessions, completed sections/chapters, meaningful annotations, book completion, exploration and long-form consistency.

## Primary resources
- **Water** — life, irrigation, population and land recovery. The signature early-game resource.
- **Coin** — construction, services, trade and upgrades.
- **Timber** — shelters, tools, fences and early buildings.
- **Stone** — wells, roads, fortifications and advanced structures.
- **Food** — population stability and settlement expansion; generated through farming rather than directly farmed by repetitive taps.
- **Knowledge** — research, blueprints, archives, special upgrades and Path-linked discoveries.
- **Influence** — trust/reputation used to attract NPCs, specialists, factions and civic upgrades.

Resource count should remain understandable. Materials may later be bundled into tiers if seven currencies creates unnecessary cognitive load.

## Settlement stages

### Tier 0 — Wasteland
Visuals: cracked earth, wind, dust, broken stones, no permanent shelter.
Unlocks: character creation, first Path choice, reading tutorial.
Goal: earn enough Water and basic material for survival.

### Tier 1 — Camp
Structures: tent, fire, water barrel, crude storage.
New systems: simple quests, first companion/NPC encounter, basic resource ledger.
Emotional beat: *I can survive here.*

### Tier 2 — Water & Soil
Structures: well/cistern, first irrigated plot, tool rack, seed bed.
New systems: land restoration meter, farming plots, first renewable resource production.
Emotional beat: *The land can live again.*

### Tier 3 — Homestead
Structures: shack/cottage, storehouse, workshop, fences, expanded fields.
New systems: crafting/blueprints, resident capacity, specialized NPC roles.
Emotional beat: *This is becoming home.*

### Tier 4 — Hamlet
Structures: several homes, mill, market stall, small library/archive, paths/roads.
New systems: population, local trade, NPC relationships, small faction presence.
Emotional beat: *Other people believe in this place.*

### Tier 5 — Village
Structures: market, smith/workshop, guild outpost, school/archive, gardens, larger farms.
New systems: civic choices, settlement specialization, multi-step quest chains, Path-influenced districts.
Emotional beat: *My reading has created a community.*

### Tier 6 — Fortified Settlement
Structures: wall, gatehouse, watchtowers, civic hall, ritual structure.
New systems: larger factions, external events, district upgrades, regional map.
Emotional beat: *The settlement can endure.*

### Tier 7 — Castle
Structures: keep, Grand Library, Ritual Chamber, Observatory, Treasury, Hidden Archive, Guild Hall, Inner Sanctum.
New systems: advanced Path progression, major rituals, artifact collections, leadership choices.
Emotional beat: *The world remembers what I built.*

### Tier 8 — City
Structures: districts, major market, expanded archives, guild quarter, gardens, workshops, monuments, upgraded walls and civic infrastructure.
New systems: district specialization, deep faction arcs, long-term city planning, endgame mysteries, prestige evolution.
Emotional beat: *A lifetime of reading became a civilization.*

## Reading reward logic
Reward types should match behaviors instead of giving every activity every currency.

- Sustained reading sessions: Water + modest XP/progression energy.
- Chapter/section completion: Coin + construction progress.
- Book/volume completion: Blueprint, artifact, major Influence or settlement milestone.
- Meaningful highlights/notes: Knowledge.
- Genre/author/format exploration: discovery tokens, rare seeds, lore or Influence.
- Returning to difficult/unfinished works: Resolve-style progression and occasional restoration bonuses.
- Series completion: landmark, district upgrade or major Chronicle entry.

## No grind rule
The game must not create a second job. Farming, workshops and production are primarily strategic/background systems unlocked by reading. The player makes choices; they do not repeatedly tap crops or timers to remain competitive.

## Settlement choices
Progression should branch. Two players with equal reading time may build different places.
Examples:
- agriculture vs trade vs knowledge focus;
- fortified vs open cultural settlement;
- Path-influenced visual districts;
- recruit one NPC specialist before another;
- invest Water in greening the land or population expansion.

Choices should be meaningful but recoverable. Avoid irreversible traps in early/mid game.

## Path influence
- **Archivist:** archives, schools, research, Knowledge efficiency.
- **Vanguard:** infrastructure, roads, fortification, difficult long-form quest bonuses.
- **Dreamwalker:** gardens, wonder structures, exploration and imaginative districts.
- **Oracle:** observatory, hidden sites, prediction/mystery systems.
- **Nocturne:** night district, cryptic monuments, rare lore and atmosphere systems.
- **Artificer:** workshops, engineering, irrigation, tools and efficiency upgrades.

No Path may lock access to core reading formats or essential settlement progression.

## Narrative integration
The reclamation itself is the main narrative metaphor: stories restore a world erased by the Veil. Books and manga leave traces in the Chronicle rather than importing copyrighted worlds directly. Original NPCs interpret the player's reading behavior and settlement history.

## Visual progression requirement
Every meaningful tier must visibly transform the same persistent map. The user should recognize old landmarks after upgrades. Prefer evolution over replacing one static screen with another.

Examples: dusty footpath → road; barrel → cistern → aqueduct; single crop patch → irrigated terraces; tent → cottage district; crude fire → town square monument.

## Economy guardrails
- No paid progression currency.
- No loot boxes.
- No energy system that blocks reading.
- No punishment for missed days.
- No FOMO construction timers.
- Offline reading must still contribute after validated session reconciliation.
- Economy should reward reading breadth/depth without making users optimize away enjoyment.

## First playable vertical slice
The first testable loop is intentionally small:
1. Start in Wasteland.
2. Complete real reading sessions.
3. Earn Water and Coin.
4. Build a tent.
5. Unlock a well/cistern.
6. Restore one farm plot.
7. Build a shack.
8. Attract the first NPC.
9. See all changes persist after restart/backup-restore.

If this loop is not emotionally satisfying, do not scale to city systems yet.

## Research questions
- What reading evidence produces rewards without encouraging fake engagement?
- What economy pace feels satisfying for light, medium and heavy readers?
- How often should the world visibly change?
- Which resources can be combined to reduce complexity?
- How much choice is enough before settlement management distracts from reading?
- What visual representation is performant and accessible on ordinary Android devices?

## Definition of success
A user should be able to say: **“I built this place because I read.”** The city is a memory of the reading journey, not a separate idle game attached to a reader.
