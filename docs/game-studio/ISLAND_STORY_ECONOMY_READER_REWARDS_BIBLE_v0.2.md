# ISLAND BOOKSHOP — Narrative, Play and Reading Progression Bible v0.2
**Status:** Creative proposal for human review, informed by actual independent Gemini 4 Argon critique, NOT canonical shipped content.
**Date:** 2026-10-14. Project branch deliberately planning-only; not merged, not implemented in game scenes.

## One-sentence game promise
A shipwreck survivor with no name gains a home above an old island bookshop, learns to care for people through the books they buy, borrow and conceal, builds a meaningful livelihood, and discovers whether the island's carefully edited history should be restored, forgiven, or rewritten together.

## Identity: cozy by day, mystery at dusk
The player experiences distinct modes within one coherent world:
- **Business craft** — run a real fictional shop with inventory, supply, condition, pricing, loans, customers, expenses, layout and upgrades. Customers have personal needs and real agency.
- **People** — connected family, friendships, enemies, grievances, romance *later if earned*, small story arcs, secrets and an elder with autonomy. Some residents do not like or trust the protagonist.
- **Island** — atmospheric detailed 16/32-pixel hand-crafted town and weather, small navigation discoveries, tide-locked areas, archives and historical contradictions.
- **Narrative portrait** — sharp original layered anime art (eyes, brows, mouth, pose, small breath) only for consequential conversations; repeat everyday exchanges use barks to preserve play rhythm.
- **Reading linkage later** — voluntary real Veil Reader reading can decorate/accelerate nonessential aspects of the game. Standalone game playable WITHOUT real reading and the book reader opens independently without game.

### What research taught us
- *Eastward*: an eccentric cast and visually dense surroundings make a small town distinctive, not just more location tiles. [Official Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/977880/)
- *Coffee Talk*: how one serves another person can drive branching narrative better than a disconnected Yes/No button. [Official Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/914800/)
- *DAVE THE DIVER*: alternate exploration and commerce; character humor and frequent varied side systems. Do not copy its exact minigames or aggressively expand scope. [Official Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1868140/)
- *Tiny Bookshop*: beautiful coastal bookstore precedent, shelf/genres/decor/recommendations. [Official Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2133760/)
- Community reactions to Tiny Bookshop are divided: some love relaxing recommendations, others report shallow repeat loops and apparently arbitrary rejected recommendations. Treat as qualitative signals, not a numeric market conclusion: [debate](https://www.reddit.com/r/CozyGamers/comments/1tbvmo9/is_getting_tiny_bookshop_worth_it/), [confusing request](https://www.reddit.com/r/cozygames/comments/1tfsf9n/small_complaint_tiny_bookshop/).
- Adapt transferable mechanics and pacing, **never duplicate another game's protected written scenes, art, characters, branded world or proprietary source code**.

## Story foundation: an original, testable mystery (candidate canon)
Working concept: **The Bookbinders of the Missing Pages** (Persian: صحافانِ صفحات گمشده).
The island's damp salt air and a rare native paper-feeding organism ruin household records. Historically, villagers kept **one repaired family chronicle** each. The bookseller is the finest binder alive and has sometimes **removed pages by consent, pressure or misguided protection**. Contradictory family histories quietly steer access to land, help, friendship and political power. The first recurring visual motif is a repaired book whose stitching holds a page from a different era.

The fog is atmospheric and an unreliable navigational problem; it does **not** grant omniscience or automatically rewrite memories. Any hallucinatory spores are a story hypothesis requiring a strict rulebook, not a magical shortcut to explain every reveal. The protagonist's post-shipwreck amnesia can be injury-related; past identity is revealed through people, objects and choices, not random cutscene flashbacks. Keep physical investigation as primary gameplay.

**Player-centered human dilemma:** The protagonist's former work may have participated in an archive confiscation that separated loved ones, but the truth must unfold from evidence rather than making the player simply a predetermined villain or chosen one. Some witnesses remember them differently, some lied for protection, and an elder's kind rescue may coexist with his concealment of evidence. The past is consequential, yet current actions—not origin or bloodline—determine reputation.

**Narrative rule:** no single NPC is purely good/evil; no final binary 'publish everything vs burn everything'. Third and fourth responses exist, e.g. negotiate conditional disclosure, consult affected families, preserve originals and share stewardship. Victims retain agency. No surprise death of the elder solely to manipulate player emotions; his illness is chronic, affects his choices and relationship but is not a timed life bar.

### Five-act story spine (proposed, each act changes hub and city)
1. **The Stranger Upstairs:** beach rescue, first shelter, learn the workbench, open shop, 2 customers; discover marginalia naming a ship missing from the harbor register. Emotional turn: elder gives the player a spare key, not ownership; trust is offered, not earned through fetching groceries.
2. **The Unpaid Debts:** supplier shortages, first loans/returns, different families ask for competing editions; player sees an apparently charitable restoration can erase someone's livelihood. Emotional turn: a regular shopper rejects help because the old shop once hurt their family.
3. **The Margins of the Town:** pier and old archive open; a clerk, scavenger and lighthouse keeper offer incompatible but plausible testimony. Shops in different districts need different genres; book lending boosts a cooperative but reduces short-term profits. Emotional turn: player realizes memories of their own involvement may have been curated.
4. **The Bookbinder's Hands:** storm threatens community records; the elder admits three specific past edits, each for a different reason (compassion, fear, selfishness). Bound journal offers real proof. Emotional turn: apology alone cannot restore all damage; community restoration must be co-designed with those affected.
5. **The Library We Leave:** player chooses a governance model for records, bookshop identity, economic priorities and a personal response to past actions. Epilogue transforms shop, town festival and NPC schedules based on cumulative decisions; no universal morality score or fake happy ending.

## Character bible seeds — 8 distinct named roles, no final name lock
All character names below are **working**: authors must preserve character motives across localized dialogue; gender/appearance of player never dictates their history.
1. **Arsalan, 68, old binder**: dry humor; proud hands shake in rain. Wants bookshop to outlive him, has suppressed old pages to protect and sometimes control. Morning loft, afternoon restoration desk, occasional dusk pier. Humorous quest: lost bag of animal-free binding glue ordered from wrong supplier; serious: restore an apology page addressed to a living relative. Relationship is trust built through disagreements, not a cheap 'father dies' timer.
2. **Leila, 34, harbor debt clerk**: rigorous public servant who keeps secret sketchbooks. Wants fair access to scarce imports, distrusts rumors, and once approved harmful requisition under pressure. Day: ledger office; evening: quay. Funny: misplaced inventory stamps appearing on fish; serious: decide which family gets rare paper during storm, with supply/restoration consequences.
3. **Nima, 21, courier with hearing aids**: quick-witted, visually observant, self-taught sign language; respected as messenger but secretly keeps unread letters. Early dock, noon shop, late rooftops. Funny: incorrectly swapped love notes cause absurd meetup; serious: deliver a letter only after sender/recipient consent. Accessibility and motivation written with authenticity review.
4. **Mahin, 52, lighthouse keeper**: meticulous former sailor afraid of deep water after loss; protects coast but spreads selective ship stories. Dawn tower, night pier. Funny: lighthouse lantern attracts seabirds stealing brass pieces; serious: contradictory official storm records and a hard decision whether to warn a dangerous incoming boat.
5. **Tara, 29, island botanist/illustrator**: bold scientist with debilitating fear of being wrong. Wants to map native plant fungus but receives funds from wealthy collectors. Midday greenhouse, evening café. Funny: an invasive seed planted in coat pockets; serious: paper-decay cure could damage fragile coastal ecology.
6. **Ramin, 40, itinerant book salvager**: charismatic, charmingly unreliable, sincere toward street kids but profits on rare scraps. Low tide wrecks, sunset market. Funny: bargaining over a fake antique; serious: choose whether to sell an anonymous child's notebook to collector or return it to family, unlocking alternate markets.
7. **Parisa, 37, tea-house owner**: funny, observant, intentionally distant; keeps customers' secrets because sanctuary depends on discretion. Afternoon and night café; early food deliveries. Funny: plant a rumor to catch a gossip; serious: decide whether to host public truth hearing while protecting one vulnerable witness.
8. **Younes, 46, community archivist**: principled, inflexible and funny when unexpectedly warm. Wants reliable public records, distrusts the bookshop's private archive, cares for estranged sibling. Workday town hall, evening home; joint quest: compare two editions, follow chain of custody, risk trust to correct a public mistake.

**Character standard before acceptance:** personal goal + contradiction + daily context + one funny and one serious event + relationship turning point + a choice that changes at least 2 later dialogues/behaviors. Never use a 'gift 5 times -> love' mechanism as substitute for writing.

## A business game with real consequences and recovery
**Core enjoyable player loop (10–15min):**
1. Receive a port delivery and inspect condition/genre/tone, evidence of prior ownership and rarity. Weather/tides affect next game's delivery in authored deterministic ways.
2. Arrange the window/shelves based on neighborhood and season; display choices influence customer demographics and NPC trust, not just passive coins.
3. A customer describes a life problem with misdirection. Player may inspect cover, read 2-line lore, repair hidden margin, talk to an NPC for context and recommend one of several plausible fictional books.
4. Choose sell at a fair price (money), lend (trust, delayed stock return), archive (clue, no cash), or preserve as private keepsake (relationship). Not every sale requires a 60-second minigame.
5. Make one serious investment decision: binding tools, extra shelf, warm reading seat, low-price community program or rare-book insurance. Each tradeoff changes customers, footfall and operating costs.
6. Close day when player chooses; receive intelligible P&L, shopper feedback and one emergent event tied to prior action, not RNG alone. At least one safe recovery path if a choice makes money low.

**Economy sources**: fictional sales, used-book buyback/refurbishment margin, paid commission repairs, community subscriptions (optional), salvage exchange, NPC barter and special exhibitions.
**Economy sinks**: stock acquisition, repair resources, shop maintenance, salary for later helper, layout upgrades and town restoration; never punitive daily real-time debt.
**Skills** (original, horizontally interesting rather than +10% generic): Archivist = compare editions; Binder = reveal inscriptions/change physical properties; Bookseller = infer customers' unspoken needs; Community Steward = secure sustainable loans and invite community workshops; Explorer = tide observation and charting. Each unlock gives an action or new decision rather than only statistics.

### Variety director (proposal requiring playtest)
Weekly game content should alternate: (A) customer dilemma with multiple valid books; (B) restoration/puzzle revealing clue; (C) social world event; (D) stocking/upgrading economic decision; (E) quiet ambient day for autonomy; (F) optional expedition. Do not force any fixed calendar or artificial task queue. NPC barks from bounded hand-authored templates never replace major authored story beats.

### Quest network principles
- Each microquest has premise, player fantasy, stakes, at least two methods, consequences, recovery/replay conditions, in-game metadata and bilingual localized scripts.
- **Example Q01 'Two Names on a Spine'**: a mother wants a family chronicle that includes her estranged daughter's maiden name; municipal registry demands sanitized edition. Sell the amended edition, lend original for discussion, or arrange a jointly witnessed restoration. The shop loses/profits money differently; later relations and a public archive route change. No single golden solution.
- **Example Q02 'The Cat Who Bought a Book'**: customer receipts repeatedly show a cat signing the shop account; reveal a dock boy using the elder's stamp to get comics for a sick sibling, decide honest subscription/relief instead of punishment. Humor that changes economics.
- **Example Q03 'The Unopened Letter'**: courier brings a sealed note hidden in binding; choosing whether to read it may reveal clue but break personal trust. Alternative: ask recipient consent or trace ink/watermark instead. Changes future access.
- **Example Q04 'The Storm Order'**: the clerk requests all blank paper for official records, while botanist needs it to publish coastal warning and elder needs repair sheets. Source alternative via salvage or redesign public notices. Changes supplies and village festival schedule.

## Optional real reading as joy, never coercive gating
**Design objective:** real time immersed in a book enhances your sense of restoring the island; do not make it a mandatory payment for story quests.

**FUTURE integration** only after standalone Godot mobile proof. One-way, opt-in aggregate reading-completion signals from existing Reader authority; never copy books, annotations, titles, personal file paths, chapter text or private URI into the game. Reader opens independently even if RPG isn't installed. TTS/slow readers and accessible modes must receive equitable credit for supported verified engagement.

**Proposed 'Archive Light' milestone examples (NOT final economy tuning, NOT implemented):**
| Tier | Milestone signal (subject to tested anti-abuse accounting) | Optional enhancement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | First voluntary verified reading session | Warm ambient shop lantern & portrait frame |
| Hearth | Cumulative reading engagement | Additional book nook decor + alternate wardrobe pigment |
| Bind | More diverse sessions OR equivalent cumulative time | Extra binding ornament, optional restoration material package |
| Neighborhood | Sustained cumulative engagement, no consecutive-day criterion | Street lanterns/garden improvements and new companion cosmetics |
| Legacy | Long-term engagement with diminishing returns | Grand archive exhibit skin, title and animated wallpaper |

**Key: story, main missions, core skills and essential economic resources remain unlockable by in-game play alone.** Reading may substitute a small amount of optional material grind or reveal **nonessential** atmospheric lore, not required clue or quest solution. Avoid 'X pages/hour' thresholds favoring fast skimmers, penalizing long books, excluding TTS, or pushing page flicking. Use cumulative eligible-time baselines and a single persisted high-water entitlement ledger to prevent double awards after process restarts/backup; explicit user choice before sharing aggregate totals with the game. Allow opt-out/reduced notifications, no streak loss or illness timers.

## Quality gates / anti-boredom experiments
- 5 real players should each finish one 15–20m session: able to state why they sold/lent the book and what their choice changed.
- At least 3 unique valid response strategies for one customer scene and 2 different outcomes later; no opaque rejection solely due to category mismatch.
- First 20m contains genuine humor, one tender non-manipulative moment, one surprising in-world clue and a demonstrated economic decision with clear feedback.
- At least 2 of 8 NPCs should actively disagree with player; no characters exist only as exposition faucets.
- Repeat 3 shop days; if 2 days are functionally identical, stop content expansion and redesign loop.
- Test player who does ZERO real reading can progress game; player who disables game can open actual EPUB/PDF at exact locator.
- No opaque daily task pileups, mandatory logging in, FOMO, multi-hour timers, gambling, pay-to-win or unearned NPC affection.

## Vertical slice: target, not promise
Week 1: validate 3 acts/characters and narrative rules; 6 book definitions with full 3-layer writing; 2 customer written needs; 1 moral microquest, 1 funny moment; 3 distinct shop-day decisions.
Week 2: greybox Godot code for choices, repair, customer reasoning, two NPC exchanges and one family ledger clue; test economy/save + localized dialogue. Only after meaningful gameplay, commission pixel town/portrait assets.
User acceptance: play 20min, answer 'I care about X and I want to find out Y', and have a reason to return independent of compulsory reading.

## Open decisions requiring collaboration or user acceptance
- Magical memory effects vs grounded edited-history mystery. Gemini suggested grounded spores/paper decay; no spore-induced memories canon until evidence/rulebook review.
- Player past: was the stranger an inspector? Avoid forcing fixed guilt or gender without owner approval.
- Elder's secrets: what did he know of player on beach? Prevent manipulative withholding except where directly confronted.
- Naming/visual bible and accessibility representation review.
- Fictional paper-decay rule, economics and closing decision must be tested against literal 8-character NPC timeline.
