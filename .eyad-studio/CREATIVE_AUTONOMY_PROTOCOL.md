# Veil Reader — Creative Autonomy Protocol

## Principle
The owner provides seeds, constraints and taste. Management is responsible for turning those seeds into a coherent product. Specialist teams are expected to discover missing systems, propose improvements and extend ideas when doing so strengthens the approved product direction.

## Team proposal lane
A team may submit an `IDEA` to Management at any time. Management converts accepted ideas into bounded missions and assigns dependencies, validation and owners.

An idea can be executed without another owner checkpoint when it is:
- aligned with the Universal Reading RPG direction;
- additive or reversible;
- implementable without new paid commitments;
- not dependent on new credentials/accounts;
- not a destructive remote operation;
- not a public/store release;
- not a major pivot away from reading-first design.

## Mandatory proposal fields
1. Player/reader value.
2. Why it belongs in Veil Reader.
3. System connections and affected teams.
4. Cost and technical risk.
5. Accessibility/privacy/performance implications.
6. Acceptance criteria.
7. Balance or abuse risks.
8. What gets delayed, simplified or removed if scope expands.

## Management decision states
- GREENLIGHT — enter the Mission Board.
- PROTOTYPE — bounded experiment first.
- MERGE — combine with an existing mission.
- PARK — keep in the idea vault.
- REJECT — conflicts with product principles or cost/value balance.

## Creative guardrails
Reading remains the primary action. Game rewards may celebrate reading but may not interrupt pages, create FOMO, sell progression, punish rest days or encourage fake page turning. Every major game system must have a Quiet Mode-compatible representation.

## Scope discipline
Autonomy is not permission for feature sprawl. New ideas must make the whole system more coherent. Management should prefer a smaller number of deeply connected systems over many isolated mechanics.
